Chemical Equilibrium

Everything here uses one reaction, making ammonia: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, which gives out heat (ΔH = −92 kJ/mol). Each kernel concept is tested by considerations. For each one, pick a rationale and write your own justification. Both are graded Perfect To some extent but not perfect or Wrong answer.

Picture it first

Two rooms joined by a doorway, with people walking both ways. After a while the number in each room stops changing, not because people stopped walking, but because as many cross each way every minute. That is equilibrium.

Kernel concepts

The 7 ideas everything is built from

K1–K3 are the basics, K4–K5 are K and Q, and K6–K7 are what happens when you disturb an equilibrium. Each consideration is tagged with the concept it tests.

#Kernel conceptCheck
K1A reversible reaction runs both ways at once: reactants form products, and products break back into reactantsAccurate. Strictly, every reaction is reversible; we call it reversible when the backward reaction is large enough to matter.
K2Equilibrium is reached in a closed system when the forward rate equals the backward rate; the amounts stop changing, but both reactions keep going (dynamic equilibrium)Accurate. 'Closed' matters: if a product escapes (a gas leaving an open flask), the backward reaction can't keep up and equilibrium is never reached.
K3At equilibrium the amounts are constant, not equal; the mixture can be mostly products or mostly reactantsAccurate. This is the most common misconception: equal rates, not equal amounts.
K4The equilibrium constant K = [products]^coefficients / [reactants]^coefficients; a large K means mostly products, a small K mostly reactants; pure solids and liquids are left outAccurate, with a qualifier: K written with concentrations is Kc (gases can also use partial pressures, Kp). K has units unless the powers cancel; for ammonia, Kc is in L²/mol².
K5The reaction quotient Q has the same form as K but uses the amounts present now: Q < K goes forward, Q > K goes backward, Q = K is equilibriumAccurate. Q is a snapshot; comparing it with K predicts the direction before anything moves.
K6Le Chatelier: when an equilibrium is disturbed by a change in concentration, pressure or temperature, it shifts to partly undo the changeAccurate. 'Partly' is important: the shift never fully cancels the change. Adding an inert gas at constant volume doesn't count as a pressure change, because no concentration changes.
K7Only temperature changes K; a catalyst speeds up both directions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner but the mixture is the sameAccurate. Temperature changes K because the forward and backward rates depend on temperature differently; a catalyst changes both by the same factor.
